# AMHOS — AI Maternal Health Operating System

### One-line positioning
AMHOS is the offline-first coordination layer that turns fragmented maternal health data — paper, SMS, siloed apps — into one continuous, risk-aware pathway from village to hospital.

---

## The Problem

Maternal and newborn deaths in much of Africa are rarely caused by a single clinical failure. They are caused by **coordination failure**: a high-risk pregnancy flagged by a community health worker (CHW) that never reaches the clinic; a referral sent by radio or phone that arrives with no context; a mother lost to follow-up in the 48 hours after birth, when most postpartum deaths occur. The tools that exist — paper registers, SMS chains, disconnected mobile apps — don't talk to each other, don't work offline, and give district health officers only retrospective, delayed data.

## The Solution

AMHOS is a mobile, web, and cloud-native platform that unifies four actors — pregnant women, CHWs, clinics/hospitals, and emergency transport — into a single real-time workflow, with AI-driven risk stratification guiding who needs attention first.

It is built offline-first from the ground up, not retrofitted: the mobile app functions fully without connectivity and syncs the moment a signal is available, so it works in the environments where maternal mortality is highest.

## What Makes This Different

Most digital health tools solve one piece — a registration app, a referral hotline, a dashboard. AMHOS is positioned as the **connective layer** across all of them:

| Instead of... | AMHOS provides... |
|---|---|
| Paper registers with no risk triage | Early, structured risk stratification at first contact |
| SMS-based referrals with no tracking | Tracked referrals with advance hospital visibility |
| Manual postpartum follow-up (or none) | Automated task orchestration through the 48-hour window |
| Quarterly retrospective reports | Live operational dashboards for district/national health teams |
| Apps that fail without signal | Full offline functionality with local queuing and sync |

## Core Value Pillars

**1. Reduce preventable mortality**
Every pregnancy is registered, triaged, and followed — closing the gap where women fall out of the system between visits.

**2. Strengthen community-to-facility coordination**
CHWs and clinical staff operate from the same live record, with automated scheduling around the highest-risk period: 0–48 hours postpartum.

**3. Work reliably where infrastructure doesn't**
Offline-first design, not a connectivity-dependent app with an offline "mode" — built for the last mile.

**4. Cut referral and transport delay**
Receiving facilities see high-risk cases coming, with timeline tracking that surfaces where transit delays occur.

**5. Give administrators real-time visibility**
District and national stakeholders get live operational data to target interventions, not lagging reports.

## Who It's For

- **Pregnant women** — continuous, trackable care instead of disconnected touchpoints
- **Community health workers** — guided task lists, risk flags, offline-capable tools
- **Clinics & hospitals** — advance warning on incoming risk, complete patient history on arrival
- **Emergency transport providers** — clear referral chains and timeline accountability
- **Ministries of Health / district health teams** — real-time operational and epidemiological visibility

## The Underlying Thesis

Africa's maternal health challenge is increasingly not a shortage of protocols or personnel — it's a shortage of **continuity**. AMHOS is not another point solution; it's the operating system that makes existing health workers, facilities, and referral systems function as one coordinated pathway.
